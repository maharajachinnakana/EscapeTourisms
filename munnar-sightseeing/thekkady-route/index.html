02 / The Route

What makes the Munnar–Thekkady route special?

The road from Munnar toward Thekkady changes gradually from high-altitude tea country to forests, reservoirs and spice-growing landscapes. Instead of spending the entire journey inside the vehicle, you can plan selected stops along the route.

03 / On The Way

Places you can explore on the way.

01

Lockhart Gap

One of the scenic viewpoints on the Munnar–Thekkady side of the route. The area around Lockhart is surrounded by tea estates and mountain valleys, making it a useful photography stop before continuing toward the lower hill ranges. Visibility depends on weather and cloud conditions.

02

Lockhart Tea Garden

Travel through tea-growing landscapes around Lockhart and the surrounding estates. This is more about the journey and landscape than a fixed-ticket attraction, so suitable roadside viewpoints can be used for photographs where stopping is safe and permitted.

03

Lockhart Tea Factory

A tea-factory visit can be added when visitor access is available. Factory entry is subject to the individual facility's operating arrangements and should be confirmed before making it a fixed part of your itinerary. Any factory-specific admission or activity charge is separate from the taxi fare.

04

Periyakanal Waterfall

Periyakanal Waterfall is located on the Munnar–Thekkady route toward Anayirankal and is particularly attractive when water flow is good. The waterfall experience can vary significantly with rainfall and season. During heavy rain, road and access conditions can also change.

05

Anayirankal Dam

Anayirankal is surrounded by tea plantations and mountain scenery and is known for its reservoir landscape. Boating may be available as a separate activity depending on current operating conditions. The reservoir area can also offer opportunities to see local wildlife, but wildlife sightings are never guaranteed.

06

Anayirankal Tea Country

The road around Anayirankal provides a different view of Munnar's landscape, with tea estates and forested hills gradually giving way to the plantation country toward Thekkady. This is one reason the Munnar–Thekkady drive works well as a sightseeing transfer rather than simply a point-to-point journey.

07

Spice Garden

As you approach Thekkady/Kumily, you can consider adding a spice-garden visit to your itinerary. Spice gardens are operated separately, so the current garden, visiting hours, admission and activities should be confirmed before booking.

08

Chathurangapara Jeep Safari

Separate activity

If you want to add an off-road experience, Chathurangapara Jeep Safari can be considered separately where available. The Jeep Safari is not included in the Munnar–Thekkady taxi fare. Safari availability and activity charges should be confirmed separately.

09

Ripple Waterfall

Optional stop

Ripple Waterfall can be considered as an optional additional stop depending on your route and available time. Because waterfall access and water flow can vary with weather and season, confirm current local conditions before adding it to a fixed itinerary.

04 / Distance & Time

How far is it — and how long does it take?

The road distance varies depending on the exact route and destination point within Thekkady/Kumily. Current route sources place the road journey broadly in the mid-80s to around 100 km, depending on the route used. A direct drive is considerably shorter than a sightseeing journey.

A non-stop road journey can take a few hours depending on the route, road conditions and traffic. A sightseeing transfer takes longer because of the stops — Lockhart Gap, waterfall stops, Anayirankal, tea-estate viewpoints, food breaks, photography and optional activities. For this reason, we recommend planning the Munnar–Thekkady transfer as a scenic travel day, rather than using a fixed drive-time promise.

Your actual travel time depends on

  • 02Weather
  • 03Road conditions
  • 04Number of sightseeing stops
  • 05Time spent at each stop
  • 06Food breaks
  • 07Optional activities

If you have a fixed check-in time at your Thekkady hotel or a scheduled activity, tell us before booking so the sightseeing plan can be kept practical.

What time should you leave Munnar?

For a sightseeing transfer, an earlier start generally gives you more time for stops before reaching Thekkady. The exact departure time should be based on your hotel checkout, desired sightseeing stops, Thekkady hotel check-in, activities booked for the evening, weather and current road conditions.

If you have a fixed activity at Thekkady, plan the taxi around that timing rather than trying to add too many sightseeing stops.

06 / Who It's For

For families, couples & groups.

Families

Families can choose a vehicle based on passenger and luggage requirements. For a family travelling with children or elderly passengers, it is usually better to select fewer, more useful stops rather than trying to visit every attraction on the route. Comfort and travel time should come before the number of stops.

Couples

Couples can turn the transfer into a relaxed scenic journey with selected viewpoints, tea gardens and waterfall stops. A private taxi gives you more flexibility than a fixed shared schedule. If photography is important, tell us before the trip so the route can allow reasonable time at scenic stops.

Groups

For larger groups, Traveller or Urbania options can help keep everyone travelling together. Vehicle selection should be based on both passenger count and luggage space.

Weather & road conditions

The Munnar–Thekkady route passes through mountainous terrain. Rain, fog, reduced visibility and road conditions can affect the journey. Waterfalls can also change significantly with rainfall.

If weather or road conditions make a particular stop unsuitable, the itinerary may need to be adjusted. Safety comes before completing every planned sightseeing stop.

Wildlife sightings

Some parts of the route pass through landscapes where wildlife can be present. You may occasionally see elephants or other wildlife around suitable forest and reservoir areas. However, wildlife sightings are never guaranteed.

Do not approach, feed, follow or disturb wild animals.
